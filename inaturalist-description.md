## Summary (short description)

Report cold-stunned and fallen green iguanas in Florida, and help us find the temperature at which iguanas lose their grip.

## Description

Every winter, a cold snap in South Florida sends green iguanas tumbling out of the trees. Most are not dead, just cold-stunned: like all lizards, an iguana's body temperature tracks its surroundings, and on a cold enough night their muscles simply stop working.

For us, each of these nights is a natural experiment. Green iguanas are native to Central and South America, so a Florida cold front pushes them right to the edge of what they can tolerate. Your observations tell us exactly where that edge is: which nights, which neighborhoods, and at what temperature iguanas let go.

**How to help**

1. Photograph any cold-stunned or fallen iguana: on the ground, in a pool, or hanging limp in a tree.
2. Upload it to iNaturalist and add it to this project.
3. Record the time you found it, and whether it was on the ground, in water, or still in the tree.
4. Leave it where it is. It will warm up and walk away (and a warmed-up, annoyed iguana in your car is a whole new problem).

Check tonight's forecast at https://jameststroud.github.io/are-iguanas-falling/

This project is run by the Stroud Lab at Georgia Tech, where we study how lizards evolve in the wild: https://www.thestroudlab.com
